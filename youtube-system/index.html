My Secret YouTube System: Create, Upload, and Grow (Even as a Beginner)

Short answer: I used to run my YouTube channel on memory and motivation. Both ran out every week. My system now is different: I let Claude run the weekly machine — idea, script, thumbnail, edit, upload, review — while I keep the decisions that actually need me.

I used to run my YouTube channel on memory and motivation. Both ran out every week. My system now is different: I let Claude run the weekly machine — idea, script, thumbnail, edit, upload, review — while I keep the decisions that actually need me. Here are my seven prompts, one for each step, in the order I use them.

1. My Weekly Upload System

My first prompt builds the machine itself. I give Claude my niche, my audience, my editing skills, my weekly hours, and my best videos — and it builds me a complete weekly pipeline: a calendar, an idea bank, a script stage, a filming day, an editing checklist, and an upload and promotion routine. One video, from idea to published, every week, no guessing.

Prompt: Act as my YouTube strategist and production manager. Build me a complete weekly upload pipeline for my channel: my niche is [NICHE], my audience is [AUDIENCE], my editing skills are [LEVEL], and I have [HOURS] hours per week. Give me a weekly calendar, an idea bank, a script stage, a filming day, an editing checklist, and an upload and promotion routine that takes each video from idea to published.

2. My Idea Bank Builder

My second prompt fills the bank. Claude studies the proven viral formats in my niche, breaks down what the top videos have in common, and gives me 30 original ideas — each scored 1 to 10 on views potential, ease, and originality. I never sit down to an empty page again.

Prompt: Act as my YouTube content researcher. Study the proven viral formats in [NICHE], analyze the top videos, and extract the patterns that make them work. Then give me 30 original video ideas, each scored 1–10 on views potential, ease, and originality.

3. My Script Engine

My third prompt turns one idea into a script people actually finish. Hook, setup, rising story, climax, payoff — in the style of my best videos, fast pacing, short sentences. Retention is built into the script, not fixed in the edit.

Prompt: Act as my YouTube scriptwriter. Turn this idea into a full retention-focused script: a strong hook, a clear setup, a rising story, a climax, and a payoff. Match the style of my best videos, keep the pacing fast, and keep the sentences short. Idea: [IDEA]

4. My Thumbnail and Title Lab

My fourth prompt handles packaging — the part that decides whether anyone clicks at all. Three thumbnail concepts with exact text, layout, colors, and emotion, plus five title options ranked by curiosity gap. I pick; I don't guess.

Prompt: Act as my YouTube packaging expert. For this video, design 3 thumbnail concepts with exact text, layout, colors, and emotion, and give me 5 title options ranked by curiosity gap. Video: [TOPIC]

5. My Editing Checklist

My fifth prompt keeps my edits tight. Based on my raw footage length, my software, my style, and where my viewers drop off, it builds me a checklist: cut order, pause-removal rules, b-roll moments, on-screen text, music cues, and a final review pass. No more bloated cuts.

Prompt: Act as my YouTube video editor. Build me an editing checklist for tight pacing. My raw footage is [LENGTH], my software is [SOFTWARE], my style is [STYLE], and my viewers usually drop off at [POINT]. Include cut order, pause-removal rules, b-roll moments, on-screen text, music cues, and a final review pass.

6. My Upload Optimizer

My sixth prompt handles the upload screen — the part most creators rush. A description whose first lines do the heavy lifting, chapter timestamps, tags, a pinned comment, and an end-screen plan that leads viewers to another one of my videos.

Prompt: Act as my YouTube upload specialist. Prepare everything for the upload screen for this video: a description with strong first lines, chapter timestamps, tags, a pinned comment, and an end-screen plan that leads viewers to another one of my videos. Video: [TOPIC]

7. My Monthly Channel Review

My seventh prompt closes the loop. Once a month, I feed Claude my numbers — click rate, average view duration, traffic sources, subscriber gain, best and worst videos — and it tells me the patterns, the reasons, the one experiment to run next month, and which videos to make more of.

Prompt: Act as my YouTube analytics coach. Review my channel's last month: click rate [X], average view duration [X], traffic sources [X], subscriber gain [X], best video [X], worst video [X]. Tell me the patterns, the reasons behind them, the one experiment I should run next month, and which videos I should make more of.

My Honest Take Before You Run This

My honest take before you run this: Claude does not literally run my channel — I still film, I still edit, I still upload. What it runs is the system around the work: the planning, the writing, the packaging, the review. That is exactly the part that used to eat my week. My rule: I run the seven prompts in order, every week, and I keep the final call on every idea, every title, and every cut.
