My 5 Claude Secret Tricks: Skills, Commands, and Subagents I Actually Use

Intro

I used to treat Claude like a search box — type a question, take the first answer, move on. Then I started noticing the difference between people who get average output from Claude and people who get work that actually holds up. The gap is not the model. It is how you talk to it.

These are the five tricks I now use every single day. None of them are complicated. All of them changed what Claude gives me back.

1. Make It Double-Check Itself — Then Grade Its Own Confidence

I always ask Claude to double and triple check its work, then grade its confidence level on the accuracy and validity of what it tells me.

It makes the answers more accurate, and just as importantly, it tells me when its information is suspect. If Claude says it is only 60% confident about a fact, I know to verify that fact before I build on it. Yes, it takes longer. The extra minute is worth more than the hour I would lose fixing a wrong answer later.

2. When You Hit the Limits, Restart With "Continue"

When you hit the limits and Claude stops working, do not start over from scratch. Restart the conversation with "Continue" once the lockdown is lifted.

Your context, your instructions, your whole thread — it is all still there. One word brings the work back instead of rebuilding it.

3. Ask It to Ask You Questions

Ask Claude to ask you questions. It helps massively.

Most bad answers come from vague prompts, not a weak model. When Claude asks me what it needs to know before answering, the final output lands far closer to what I actually wanted — because I answered the right questions before it started writing.

4. Graduate to Skills

Once you find yourself pasting the same multi-step instructions into a Project more than once, that is your signal to turn it into a reusable Skill.

If I am repeating the same instructions for the third time, I am wasting my own time. A Skill turns that repeated setup into something I invoke in one line — same quality, zero repetition.

5. Stop Wasting Your Precious Opus Usage

Stop wasting your precious Opus usage. I use Claude for high-level architecture and planning, then have it build me local tools — or I use a cheaper local model like Qwen for the actual coding and repetitive tasks.

Opus is my architect, not my labourer. The expensive model designs the system; the cheap model writes the repetitive code. Use the right tool for the job, and the usage limits stop being a problem.
