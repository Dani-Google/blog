My New Secret Weapon: OpenMontage — Free, Unlimited AI Videos?

Short answer: I have spent years learning to edit. So when I first heard about OpenMontage, my honest reaction was the same one every editor is having right now: what exactly am I still needed for?

I have spent years learning to edit. So when I first heard about OpenMontage, my honest reaction was the same one every editor is having right now: what exactly am I still needed for? Here is my first look at the free open-source system that shot to the top of GitHub trending — and why I think every short-form creator should be watching it.

1. My Discovery: What OpenMontage Is

My understanding is simple: OpenMontage is an open-source intelligent video production system. Not a plugin, not a template pack — a full pipeline. You give it one plain sentence, and it runs the whole job: it writes the script, gathers the footage, records the voiceover, adds the subtitles, and delivers a finished video. Nothing in the middle needs my hands.

2. My Favorite Trick: Feed It a Video You Like

The feature that stopped me: I can hand it a video I already like, and it breaks down that video's structure and pacing, then rebuilds a complete production plan from it. That is not copying a clip — it is learning the architecture underneath a video that already works, and reusing the blueprint.

3. My Honest Take: Should Editors Worry?

Here is my honest take. A tool that does the whole pipeline will absolutely change what "editing" means — the mechanical assembly is getting automated. But the parts that still need a human are the parts I care about most: taste, story, and judgment. My plan is simple: I test tools like this early, I learn what they do well, and I keep the creative decisions mine. The editors who lose are the ones who ignore it; the ones who win are the ones who direct it.

My Rule Before I Judge It

My rule before I judge any new tool: I test it first. OpenMontage is a new open-source project, and I have not run it through a full production test myself yet — so I treat the hype as a signal to test, not a verdict. That is what I plan to do with it, and if it survives my test, it earns a place in my workflow.
