I spent months thinking YouTube was the only home for my AI short films. Then I started looking for platforms built for the kind of films I actually make — short, cinematic, AI-generated — and I found five that pay creators directly. There is still no true Netflix-style catalogue for independent AI shorts, but these five are the closest thing I have found so far.

My First Discovery: Cinee Pays Me Like a Filmmaker, Not a Follower Count

Cinee lets me upload my finished AI films and earn through pay-per-view, subscriptions, tips, and licensing. I keep around 70% of what my films earn, payouts come weekly, and there is no follower minimum — my films earn on their own merit, not on how big my audience already is. For me, that last part matters most, because my work should speak before my numbers do.

Why Generated Worlds Caught My Eye for Short Dramas

Generated Worlds is built for AI short dramas, and it offers me 80% of the revenue. Publishing is free, and viewers unlock episodes one by one — which fits exactly how I think about serialized stories. If my short drama hooks someone in episode one, the platform's model turns that attention into income.

How I See HUGE: A Marketplace Plus Contests

HUGE runs a marketplace where I keep 80% of my earnings, and it adds contests on top. I like the contest angle because it gives my films a stage even before they have an audience — a good film can get discovered on merit, not just on marketing.

What Vurt Taught Me About Vertical Films

Vurt accepts approved vertical films and splits ad revenue 50/50 with me. The approval step means not everything gets in, and I actually respect that — a curated feed protects the value of the films that do make it. My vertical shorts finally have a place that treats them as films, not just clips.

My Animated Shorts Found Their Home on Creator Animation

Creator Animation does the same kind of deal for animated shorts, at 80% for me. My animated work lives in a different world from my live-action-style films, so having a platform that understands animation specifically makes a real difference to how my work is presented and found.

My Honest Take: The Netflix of AI Shorts Does Not Exist Yet

I will be straight with you — none of these five is the open Netflix-style catalogue I dream about for independent high-quality AI shorts. Each one covers a piece of the puzzle: one pays best, one fits dramas, one runs contests, one curates vertical, one champions animation. My strategy is simple: I make the film once, then I place it where it earns best and gets seen by the right people. And I always check the current terms before I upload anywhere, because these young platforms change fast.

My AI films deserve more than one shelf. YouTube will always be part of my plan, but it is no longer my only plan. If you are making AI shorts too, look beyond the obvious — your best-paying audience might be waiting on a platform you have never heard of yet.
