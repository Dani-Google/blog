I Stopped Paying for a Video Team: 12 Claude Skills That Build My Entire Pipeline

Short answer: For years, I believed a proper video needed a team. Someone to draw the storyboard. Someone for the VFX.

For years, I believed a proper video needed a team. Someone to draw the storyboard. Someone for the VFX. Someone for the sound. Someone to sit in the edit and stitch it all together. That was simply the price of quality, and solo creators like me paid it in time, in money, or in lower quality.

Then I came across a stack that changed my mind: twelve Claude skills, all running inside Claude Code, that cover the entire pipeline from script to final edit. I have not replaced my whole workflow overnight. But I can see exactly where each piece fits, and that is what this article is about.

The pipeline in one line

Script → storyboard → shots → VFX → sound → edit.

That is the whole job. Every skill below plugs into one of those six steps. Nothing here is a magic button. The real story is more interesting than magic: the model writes code that draws every frame, then checks its own work. Most "one prompt" videos were never one prompt. They were a system.

The twelve skills

Video: turning words into moving pictures

1. HyperFrames — Write HTML, get a video. It now ships free music, SFX, stock footage, and text-to-speech. https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes

2. Remotion Skills — Claude cuts real videos in React. https://github.com/remotion-dev/skills

Story: the part that decides everything

3. Cinematic Director — Script in, shot list and keyframe prompts out. https://github.com/wuwangzhang1216/DirectorSKILL

4. Drama Skills — Idea to episodes, character sheets, and storyboards. https://github.com/zenstory-ai/drama-skills

5. Seedance Storyboard — Story to storyboards to Seedance 2.0 shots. https://github.com/liangdabiao/Seedance2-Storyboard-Generator

6. Claude Animated Shorts — Viral animated shorts, 2D characters in a 3D world. https://github.com/mromidev/claude-animated-shorts

VFX: the look

7. PixelFX — 244 pixel effects in code, plus a skill that makes more. https://github.com/patch-gg/pixelfx

8. Shader Skills — GLSL, Shadertoy, SDF raymarching, water and clouds. https://github.com/gokayfem/shader-skills

9. WebGPU Skill — Three.js WebGPU, TSL, and compute particles. https://github.com/dgreenheck/webgpu-claude-skill

10. ComfyUI MCP — Claude builds and runs your ComfyUI graph. https://github.com/artokun/comfyui-mcp

Sound: the half people forget

11. ElevenLabs Skills — Official. Sound effects, music, and voices. https://github.com/elevenlabs/skills

12. AI Music Skills — A full album pipeline on Suno. https://github.com/bitwize-music-studio/claude-ai-music-skills

What surprised me most

Two things. First, how complete the coverage is. There is no gap in the pipeline where I still need to hire someone. Sound was always my weak spot, and the ElevenLabs skills cover effects, music, and voices in one place.

Second, the honesty hiding inside the hype. The creator of this stack is open about it: the videos are not one prompt. The model writes code, renders frames, looks at its own output, and fixes it. That is a workflow, not a miracle — and workflows can be learned, repeated, and improved. Miracles cannot.

How I would actually start

I would not install all twelve at once. I would start with one: HyperFrames. It is the fastest way to feel the pipeline working, because you write something simple in HTML and get a video back, with music and sound already in it. One video, one skill, one evening. Then I would add the Cinematic Director next, because my own rule has always been the same: storyboard and script approved first, then production. A shot list before a single frame is rendered is exactly how I already work — this just makes that step faster.

The Seedance Storyboard skill is the one I am watching closest, because Seedance is already part of my workflow. Story to storyboard to Seedance shots, in one chain, is the piece that could save me the most time.

The honest caveats

The skills themselves are free on GitHub. The workflow is not free: it runs on Claude Code, which needs a paid Claude plan, and the heavy work runs on Opus. That is the real cost, and it is worth knowing before you start.

And none of this removes the need for taste. A bad story with beautiful VFX is still a bad video. The stack removes the team. It does not remove the thinking. The advantage now goes to the person with the best story, not the biggest budget — and that is the best news a solo creator has had in years.

The takeaway

A video team used to be the price of entry. Now it is an option. Twelve skills, one pipeline, and the same rule I have always followed: story first, frames second. I am starting with HyperFrames this week, one video, and I will see where it takes me.
