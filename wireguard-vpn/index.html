Free VPNs Are Dead. I Built My Own in 10 Minutes.

Short answer: I used to think a free VPN was a good deal. Then I learned the deal: if you're not paying, you're the product. Free VPNs sell your data, throttle your speed, and crowd thousands of strangers onto the same IP — the same IP that gets flagged everywhere.

I used to think a free VPN was a good deal. Then I learned the deal: if you're not paying, you're the product. Free VPNs sell your data, throttle your speed, and crowd thousands of strangers onto the same IP — the same IP that gets flagged everywhere.

So I built my own. It took about 10 minutes, and it runs on WireGuard.

What it is

WireGuard is a modern VPN protocol — faster and simpler than OpenVPN. And wireguard-install, a free script on GitHub by hwdsl2, turns a small server into your personal VPN: it installs WireGuard, generates your configs, and gives you a QR code to scan on your phone.

Why it beats a free VPN

  • Your own IP. Nobody else uses it, so it doesn't get flagged for someone else's abuse. For me, this is the real win — a static IP that's mine alone.
  • No logs by a VPN company. Your traffic goes through a server you control.
  • Speed. WireGuard is lightweight — no bloated app, no crowded server.
  • One config works everywhere. Phone, laptop, tablet — same setup.

Step 1: Get a small VPS

Rent the cheapest VPS you can find — around $3–5/month — with Ubuntu 22.04.

Step 2: Run the installer

SSH into the server as root and run:

curl -O https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hwdsl2/wireguard-install/master/wireguard-install.sh
chmod +x wireguard-install.sh
./wireguard-install.sh

The script asks a few questions (port, DNS, client name) and does the rest. Time: about 5 minutes.

Step 3: Add your devices

The script shows a QR code — scan it with the WireGuard app on your phone. For your laptop, it saves a config file — import it into the WireGuard app. One command adds more devices later.

Step 4: Connect and check

Turn the VPN on, then check your IP — it should show your server's IP. Done. You now have your own private tunnel.

The honest catch

  • It's not free. The server costs a few dollars a month. Still cheaper than most paid VPNs.
  • Your VPS provider can see your traffic. Pick a reputable one. You're trading trust in a VPN company for trust in a hosting company.
  • Datacenter IPs can still be flagged by strict sites. This is a privacy tool, not a magic invisibility cloak.
  • You're the admin now. Updates are on you — a few minutes a month.

Bottom line

If you use a VPN every day, stop renting someone else's crowded, logging server. Build your own. Ten minutes, a few dollars a month, and the IP is yours alone.
