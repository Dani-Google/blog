Rakuten Affiliate + Muse: A $300–$500/Month Roadmap — and the 3 Prompts That Run It

Short answer: There is a simple idea going around: one affiliate program, one AI tool, one social channel — and a monthly income claim of $300 to $500 attached to it. The program is Rakuten Affiliate.

There is a simple idea going around: one affiliate program, one AI tool, one social channel — and a monthly income claim of $300 to $500 attached to it. The program is Rakuten Affiliate. The tool is Muse. The channel is X and Threads.

I looked at the parts I could actually check, because "$300–$500 a month" is exactly the kind of number that gets shared without proof. Here is what the method is, the three prompts it runs on — copied exactly, word for word — and the three problems nobody in the discussion has solved yet.

What is Rakuten Affiliate?

Rakuten Affiliate works like Amazon Associates. Rakuten gives you links to its products, you promote those links on your blog or social accounts, and you earn a commission when someone buys through them.

The pitch is that you do not need a website, a store, or stock. You need three things: a Rakuten Affiliate account, a Muse account, and an audience on X or Threads that actually buys the kind of products you promote.

That last part matters more than the first two, and I will come back to it.

The whole system in 3 steps

The method is a loop, not a single trick:

Step 1: Research. Ask Muse to find the 10 best-selling products on Rakuten Affiliate in your category, check what is trending on X, read the reviews, and pick the top 3 most likely to sell.

Step 2: Create. Have Muse write 30 posts — 10 product posts and 20 relatable, helpful ones — and add them to your Google Calendar on a 30-day schedule.

Step 3: Analyze. Every week, feed Muse your numbers — impressions, likes, clicks, conversions, earnings — and let it tell you what sold, what flopped, and what to change next week.

Then repeat. Research, create, analyze. That is the whole roadmap.

How to use them

Step 1: Fill in the three 〇〇 lines in Prompt 1 — your category, your target audience, and your platform (X or Threads). Paste it into Muse and let it do the research. The prompt tells it to use only verified facts from the past 30 days, to include source URLs, and never to fabricate prices or reviews. Check the top 3 it picks before you promote anything — if the prices or links look wrong, they probably are.

Step 2: Fill in the three 〇〇 lines in Prompt 2 — the product, 10 phrases from real reviews, and your own experience using it. Paste it into Muse. It writes 30 posts of 140 characters or fewer, marks the product posts with "PR", and builds the 30-day schedule. Read every post before it goes on the calendar — the prompt forbids adding anything before your approval, and that rule is there for a reason.

Step 3: Every week, paste your real numbers into Prompt 3 — impressions, likes, clicks, conversions, earnings per post. Muse finds your top 3 posts, calculates your click-through and conversion rates, and gives you three improvements and five new ideas for next week. If a number is missing, it writes "Cannot be analyzed" instead of guessing. That is the most useful line in the whole prompt.

The honest caveats

Three problems, stated plainly, because the $300–$500 claim means nothing until these are answered.

First, the payment problem is real and unsolved. Rakuten Affiliate pays commission in Rakuten Cash, and a bank transfer needs a Japanese bank account and a Japanese ID. If you are not in Japan, that is a wall. The post says exchangers can solve it — but it does not name a single exchanger, and nobody in the discussion explained how one works or what it costs. Until someone shows a real payout reaching a Pakistani bank account, treat the income claim as unproven.

Second, you need a Japanese audience. Rakuten is a Japanese marketplace. Your buyers are mostly Japanese shoppers on X and Threads. If your followers are not Japanese, or you have no followers at all, the links have nobody to sell to. One commenter asked exactly this — with zero followers, is it even possible? Nobody answered with proof.

Third, the tool itself is a question mark. The method assumes you have a working Muse account. In the discussion, some people said Muse is not officially available in Pakistan, while others were selling accounts for 100–200 rupees. Buying a shared or resold account for an AI service is a good way to lose access mid-month, along with whatever work you built on it. If you try this, use an official account, not a resold one.

And the obvious one: $300–$500 a month is a claim, not a result. No earnings screenshot, no payout proof, no timeline. Affiliate income is commission per sale — no sales, no income, no matter how good the prompts are.

Who should try this?

Try it if you already have — or can build — a Japanese audience on X or Threads, and you can solve the payment question before you invest weeks in it. The prompts themselves are solid: the research prompt forces real sources, the content prompt forbids fake claims, and the analysis prompt refuses to guess when data is missing. That is better prompt design than most "make money with AI" posts.

Skip it for now if you have no Japanese audience and no payment route. In that case the prompts are still worth saving — the research, content, and analysis pattern works for any affiliate program, including ones that pay in your own country.

The prompts cost nothing to keep. The expensive part is spending a month promoting products nobody can buy from you.
